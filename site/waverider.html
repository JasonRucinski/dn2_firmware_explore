Digitone II Mods / waverider

Waverider

A sixth synth machine for the Digitone II. Two wavetable oscillators, a sub-oscillator and a noise generator, with MOVE to sweep each oscillator through its table. Tables come from DNX and live on the +Drive.

Build it

The image is rebuilt, re-signed with the key derived from your own file, and every integrity field is checked again. Nothing downloads unless all of them pass.

You will need DNX

DNX runs in your browser too. It backs up the +Drive, puts wavetables on it from WAV files, manages each project's pool, and removes Waverider's data when you go back to stock.

Then, on the unit

  1. Back up the +Drive with DNX.
  2. Open Elektron Transfer, connect the Digitone II over USB and drop the patched file onto it. The unit updates and restarts.
  3. MACHINE SEL lists WAVERIDER, after SWARMER.

Nothing else needs setting up. The +Drive has no wavetables until you add one with DNX; the first table you add with DNX sets up the storage.

Before you start

  • Digitone II OS 1.11 only.
  • Try it alone first, on a new project. If something goes wrong, that tells us it is the new machine and not a combination.
  • When you report a problem, say which mods your file has.
  • If the unit does not boot: hold FUNC at power on, open the Early Start-up Menu and upgrade with the stock 1.11 file over MIDI DIN.

Choosing it

WAVERIDER is in MACHINE SEL, after SWARMER. A Waverider track has five SYN pages; the header reads Waverider (1/5) to (5/5).

Pages 1 and 2: the oscillators

Both pages have the same controls. Page 1 is oscillator 1, page 2 is oscillator 2.

KnobControlWhat it does
ATUNE / DETNPitch in semitones. DETN is the offset from oscillator 1; TUNE moves both
BLEVLevel, 0 to 127. 100 is unity
CPOSPosition in the table, 0 to 120
DTBLThe table: Prim., Harm., then T:001 and up for the pool
ERATEHow fast MOVE runs, 0 to 100. With SYN1 or SYN2 on it reads as a note length
FMLEVHow much MOVE lowers the level, 0 to 127. 0 is none
GMPOSHow far MOVE moves the position, 0 to 100. 50 is none
HMOVEThe shape MOVE follows, one of eleven

The wave shows one voice at a time, not all of them: usually the newest note, otherwise the first voice it picked. Every voice is still computed and heard. One cursor per note is under Coming next.

MOVE shapes: Ramp Up, Ramp Down, Exp Up, Exp Down, Tri Once, Up Loop, Down Loop, Tri Loop, Square, Rnd Hold, Rnd Glide. The first five stop at their end; the rest repeat.

Page 3: sub and noise

KnobControlWhat it does

BOCT-1, -2 or -3 octaves below the oscillator it follows
CWAVESIN, TRI, SQR or PLS (a 25 % pulse)
DSRCFollows OSC1 or OSC2

FTYPEWHT, PNK, BRN or DIG

The middle of the page draws the sub and the noise.

Page 4: sound options

The middle of the page shows the track's sound as a scope.

Page 5: Shape

FOLD, WARP, FM and NPOS on top; TYPE, AMT, P1 and P2 below. They are reserved for the sound features that come next and do not change the sound yet. You can set and p-lock them. The middle of the page shows the scope, as on page 4.

The wavetable pool

  • Tables: 1 to 64 frames of 64 to 4096 points each, in powers of two. Two are built in, Prim. and Harm.
  • Names: the header names the table as you turn TBL, as "T:004 name".
  • Adding and removing: with DNX, or from the wavetable page on the unit (press PRESET/KIT twice). A project keeps its own pool of up to 128 tables.
  • Your project file stays a normal project: the pool is kept beside the project on the +Drive, not inside it, so stock firmware can still load a project you saved. Its Waverider tracks come up as FM Tone, with their settings read as FM Tone's, so they sound different. Checked in the emulator; not yet on an instrument.
  • An empty pool: a project with no pool plays no wavetable. CREATE NEW starts that way, and so does a project saved before pools existed: its Waverider tracks play Prim. until you add tables.
  • A deleted table: its pool slots read MISSING and play Prim. They keep the slot until you CLEAR SLOT it, so a sound set to that slot never jumps to another table on its own.
  • Replacing a table while it plays: the old table keeps playing until the new one is ready, then it crossfades into the new one over the DCLK time. A slot whose first table is still loading plays Prim. until it is ready.

P-locks, LFOs and saving

  • P-locks: every control on pages 1 to 5 can be p-locked. The locks stay through a reboot and through SAVE and reload.
  • LFO destinations: every control except Shape's TYPE, AMT and P1 is in the LFO's DEST list, in two groups both headed SYN.

Modulation on the page

When an LFO or MOVE moves POS or TBL, the page shows the value you hear beside the value you set. Only POS and TBL, on both oscillators, show it; every other control shows the value you set. Nothing extra is drawn while a control is not moving, and the range fades about two seconds after the movement stops.

  • Slow, under 3 Hz: a dot under POS at the value heard, a dotted line over the range it sweeps, and a dotted cursor under the position bar. The wave is the frame you hear.
  • Medium, 3 to 12 Hz: the dot over a grey band. The wave shows the two frames at the ends of the sweep.
  • Fast, over 12 Hz: the band and the two frames, no dot, and ≈ beside POS.
  • TBL: the wave shows the table you hear; the label stays on the table you set.

Why 3 and 12 Hz: the page redraws up to 24 times a second. A dot moves smoothly up to an eighth of that, 3 Hz, and jerkily up to half, 12 Hz. Faster than half the redraw rate, a moving dot would show a false, slower motion, so past 12 Hz the page shows only the range.

Captured in the emulator. It runs no LFOs, so the value heard was written in by hand. The scopes on pages 4 and 5 show Waverider's own output, rendered outside the instrument, since the emulator makes no sound.

Going back to stock

  1. In DNX, back up the device, then use Remove all Waverider data in Settings. It removes every stored table and every pool list from the +Drive, and saves each table as a file first.
  2. Flash the stock 1.11 file.

Do step 1 first: stock 1.11 cannot reach the wavetable region, so once it is on, the data stays where it is. Your projects, presets, kits and samples are not touched. A project saved with Waverider tracks stays on the unit.

Known limits in this alpha

  • Page 5 does not change the sound yet.
  • A pool reload while a note plays on a pool table may click. Tell us if you hear it.
  • 4096-point tables have not been measured on the unit.
  • A sound made before page 5 existed shows FOLD, WARP, FM and NPOS at 64.

Performance

A 16-note chord on Waverider loads the DSP about as much as FM Tone does: 65.0 % against 62.9 %. Each bar is the median load on a Digitone II, one-second readings, 16 voices.

WaveTone58.0 %
FM Tone62.9 %
FM Tone, heavy patch63.1 %
Waverider, Prim.64.8 %
Waverider, 64x2048 table65.0 %

The bars run from 0 to 100 %. Waverider measured 2026-10-12, both oscillators on, sub and noise as in the test project. The factory machines measured 2026-10-09 in an earlier project. Ten minutes on the 64x2048 table stayed flat at 65.0 %, with no overruns.

How it is made

  • The sound is made on the DSP. Waverider adds its own code to the Digitone II's sound processor; the main processor runs its pages, the pool and the +Drive.
  • The reader. Each table is kept in several versions, each with fewer harmonics for higher notes (mip-maps), and read with a smooth curve between points (Hermite). High notes stay clean without aliasing.
  • Tables are built on the unit. When a pool loads, the DSP builds each table's versions in its spare time: about 0.16 s for a 64x2048 table. Until then that slot plays Prim.
  • The +Drive store. Tables live in a region of the +Drive that the factory firmware's code does not read or write. Two copies of its index are kept, so a write cut short leaves the last good one.
  • Level. TRIM starts at -11 dB, so a new Waverider sound is as loud as a new WaveTone.
