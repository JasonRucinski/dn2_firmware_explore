Waverider

A sixth synth machine for the Digitone II. Waverider has two wavetable oscillators, a sub-oscillator and a noise generator. MOVE sweeps each oscillator through its table. You put the tables on the +Drive with DNX.

  1. Back up the +Drive with DNX.
  2. Open Elektron Transfer.
  3. Connect the Digitone II to the computer with a USB cable.
  4. Drag the patched file onto the Digitone II in Transfer. The unit updates and starts again.

Select Waverider

WAVERIDER is in MACHINE SEL, after SWARMER. A Waverider track has five SYN pages. The header shows Waverider (1/5) to Waverider (5/5).

Pages 1 and 2: the oscillators

Page 1 is for oscillator 1. Page 2 is for oscillator 2. The two pages have the same controls.

BLEVThe level, 0 to 127. At 100, the gain is 1

The wave shows only one voice. Usually, this is the voice of the newest note. If not, it is the first voice that the page selected. The unit calculates all the voices, and you hear all of them. One cursor for each note is in "Plans".

There are eleven MOVE shapes. These five shapes stop at their end: Ramp Up, Ramp Down, Exp Up, Exp Down and Tri Once. These six shapes repeat: Up Loop, Down Loop, Tri Loop, Square, Rnd Hold and Rnd Glide.

Page 5: Shape

The top row has FOLD, WARP, FM and NPOS. The bottom row has TYPE, AMT, P1 and P2. These controls are for sound functions in a future version. They do not change the sound now. You can set and p-lock them. The middle of the page shows the scope, as on page 4.

The wavetable pool

  • Tables: each table has 1 to 64 frames. Each frame has 64 to 4096 points, in powers of two. Waverider has two built-in tables: Prim. and Harm.
  • Names: when you turn TBL, the header shows the table name, for example "T:004 name".
  • Add and remove tables: use DNX, or use the wavetable page on the unit. To open the wavetable page, push PRESET/KIT two times. Each project has its own pool of up to 128 tables.
  • Your project file is a normal project: the pool is on the +Drive, next to the project and not in it. Because of this, the stock firmware can load a project that you saved. Its Waverider tracks show as FM Tone, and the stock firmware reads their settings as FM Tone settings. Thus they have a different sound. We examined this in the emulator only, not on an instrument.
  • An empty pool: a project with no pool has no pool tables. Its Waverider tracks play Prim. until you add tables. A new project (CREATE NEW) has an empty pool. A project that you saved before you installed Waverider also has an empty pool.
  • A deleted table: its pool slot shows MISSING and plays Prim. The slot stays until you clear it with CLEAR SLOT. Because the slot stays, a sound that uses the slot does not change to a different table.
  • When you replace a table that plays: the old table continues to play until the new table is ready. Then the old table crossfades into the new table, in the DCLK time. If the first table of a slot is not ready, the slot plays Prim. until the table is ready.

The wavetable page

Push PRESET/KIT two times. The left column has three lists: LOAD, MANAGE and POOL. The right column shows the pool of this project. IN POOL is the number of tables in the pool. FREE is the number of empty slots. +DRIVE is the number of tables on the +Drive.

  • Move: push UP or DOWN, or turn LEVEL/DATA. Hold FUNC and push UP or DOWN to move 7 rows.
  • Open and close: push YES to open a list. Push NO to go back. Push PRESET/KIT or NO on the wavetable page to close it.
  • Operations: in a list, push RIGHT. Push UP or DOWN to select an operation, then push YES. An operation applies to the ticked rows. If no row is ticked, it applies to the highlighted row. Push NO or LEFT to close the operations.
  • LOAD: the slots of the pool. Select a slot and push YES. If the last SYN page that you saw is page 2, the table goes to TBL2. If not, it goes to TBL1. RIGHT shows LOAD TO TBL1 and LOAD TO TBL2. On a track that is not a Waverider track, LOAD changes nothing.
  • MANAGE: all the tables on the +Drive. A + shows a table that is in the pool. Push YES to tick a table. ADD TO POOL puts the ticked tables in empty slots. DELETE removes the ticked tables from the +Drive, after you confirm with YES.
  • POOL: the slots of the pool. CLEAR SLOT empties the ticked slots. A MISSING slot stays until you clear it.
  • Empty slots show a line of slashes.

P-locks, LFOs and SAVE

  • P-locks: you can p-lock all the controls on pages 1 to 5. The p-locks stay after a reboot, and after SAVE and LOAD.
  • LFO destinations: all the controls except TYPE, AMT and P1 on page 5 are in the DEST list of the LFO. The controls are in two groups. Each group has the header SYN.

Modulation on the page

When an LFO or MOVE changes POS or TBL, the page shows the value that you hear next to the value that you set. Only POS and TBL show this, on the two oscillators. All other controls show only the value that you set. When a control does not move, the page shows nothing more. The page removes the range about two seconds after the movement stops.

  • Slow, less than 3 Hz: a dot under POS shows the value that you hear. A dotted line shows the range of the sweep. A dotted cursor is under the position bar. The wave is the frame that you hear.
  • Medium, 3 to 12 Hz: the dot is on a grey band. The wave shows the two frames at the ends of the sweep.
  • Fast, more than 12 Hz: the page shows the band and the two frames, but no dot. The symbol ≈ is next to POS.
  • TBL: the wave shows the table that you hear. The label shows the table that you set.

The reason for 3 Hz and 12 Hz: the page updates the screen up to 24 times a second. Up to 3 Hz (1/8 of that rate), the dot moves smoothly. Up to 12 Hz (1/2 of that rate), the dot moves in steps. Above 12 Hz, a dot that moves would show an incorrect, slower movement. For this reason, above 12 Hz the page shows only the range.

We made these captures in the emulator. The emulator does not run LFOs. For this reason, we wrote the value that you hear into the capture by hand. The emulator makes no sound. For this reason, the scopes on pages 4 and 5 show Waverider output that we rendered outside the instrument.

Go back to the stock firmware

Before you start, check these three conditions in DNX:

  • You use Chrome or Edge. DNX needs one of them to save files into a folder.
  • A DNX folder is set. To set it, click settings in the tool row, then DNX folder, then Choose…. DNX saves one file for each table there. Without a folder, DNX does not start the erase.
  • WRITE is on, in the tool row at the top.
  1. In DNX, click settings in the tool row. Back up and Erase work from every tool except the probe.
  2. Under Back up the +Drive, click Back up…. This backup holds your projects, sounds and kits. It does not hold the wavetables.
  3. Under Erase the wavetable region, click Erase….
  4. DNX asks “Save a copy of N wavetable(s) first?”. Click Save the copies. On an empty store, the button reads Carry on.
  5. Wait while DNX saves each table as a file in the DNX folder. On a full store, this takes minutes. These files are the backup of your wavetables.
  6. DNX shows the counts and the names of what it will erase. Click Erase the region. DNX erases all the stored tables and all the pools, then reads the instrument again.
  7. Flash the stock 1.11 file.

Do steps 1 to 6 before step 7. The stock firmware 1.11 cannot get access to the wavetable region. If you flash the stock firmware first, the data stays on the +Drive. To remove it, flash Waverider again, do steps 1 to 6, and then flash the stock firmware again.

The erase does not change your projects, presets, kits and samples. A project that you saved with Waverider tracks stays on the unit.

To put a saved table back later, with Waverider on the unit:

  1. In DNX, open the library.
  2. In the list next to Browse, select Wavetables.
  3. Drag the saved file onto a free slot in the +Drive wavetables list. This only stages the table. DNX does not send it yet.
  4. Make sure that WRITE is on.
  5. Click Write staged tables.

How it works

  • The DSP makes the sound. Waverider adds its code to the sound processor (DSP) of the Digitone II. The main processor runs the pages, the pool and the +Drive access.
  • The reader. The unit keeps each table in a set of versions (mip-maps). For higher notes, a version has fewer harmonics. The reader uses a smooth curve between the points (Hermite). Because of this, high notes have no aliasing.
  • The unit builds the tables. When a pool loads, the DSP builds the versions of each table, in time that it does not use for the sound. A 64x2048 table takes about 0.16 s. Until the table is ready, its slot plays Prim.
  • The +Drive store. The tables are in a region of the +Drive that the factory firmware does not read or write. The store keeps two copies of its index. If a write stops before its end, the last good copy stays.
  • Level. The default TRIM is -11 dB. At this value, a new Waverider sound has the same loudness as a new WaveTone sound.

Technical

This tab is for people who make tables, and for people who want to know how Waverider makes its sound. A script computes every figure and every table on this tab from the code that runs on the unit (scripts/gen_waverider_tech.py). Nobody typed the numbers.

The tables that Waverider plays

  • A table has 1 to 64 frames. A frame is one cycle of a wave: 64 to 4,096 points, in powers of two. A table is mono.
  • On the +Drive, a table is 16-bit samples, frame after frame. DNX scales each table to full scale (a peak of ±32,767) when it writes the table. The unit does not change the level back.
  • No sample rate. A frame is one cycle, so the note sets the pitch. The unit does not resample a table. A table outside the limits above stays on the +Drive and plays Prim.
  • The two built-in tables, Prim. and Harm., are 16 frames of 512 points. Prim. is a saw that becomes a sine. Harm. steps through the overtone series, partials 1 to 16.
  • In the DSP, each pool slot has its own memory region of 2,052 KiB. When a pool loads, the DSP makes the mip levels of each table in its spare time (below). A 64 × 2,048 table takes about 0.16 s. Until a table is ready, its slot plays Prim.

The files that DNX accepts

  • WAV files: DNX reads them exactly, with no change to the samples. DNX reads 8, 16, 24 and 32-bit integer PCM and 32 and 64-bit float, also in the extensible WAV format. A compressed WAV is refused.
  • Other formats (for example FLAC or MP3): the browser decodes them. The browser can resample, which changes the number of samples. DNX tells you when this occurs.
  • Channels: DNX mixes them to mono (the average). If the mix loses more than half of the peak, DNX warns you.
  • The sample rate of the file does not matter. Only the number of samples matters.

The shape of the table. A file is only a list of samples, so DNX must guess how many frames it holds. DNX tries these frame lengths in this order: 2,048, 1,024, 4,096, 512, 256, 128 and 64 points. It proposes the first length that divides the file and gives 1 to 64 frames. You can change waves (the number of frames) and points before DNX writes the table.

When the file does not divide, DNX resamples it to fit. A frame that is a power-of-two cycle is resized through a Fourier transform, so the resize adds no aliasing. blend keeps the frames of the file and mixes neighbouring frames to make the number of frames that you set.

How Waverider reads a table

From POS to a frame

POS goes from 0 to 120 for every table. The unit spreads the frames of the table over that range. A 16-frame table moves one frame every 8 steps of POS. A 64-frame table moves one frame about every 1.9 steps. An LFO and MOVE move POS in steps of 1/256 of a POS step.

Between two frames

When POS is between two frames, the reader mixes the two frames, sample by sample, in the proportion of POS. The DSP calculates POS once a block: a block is 32 samples, 1,500 blocks a second at 48 kHz.

Harm., frames 1 and 2, and the mix at POS 12, halfway between them.

The +Drive index has a flag for "no interpolation between waves". The alpha does not use it. It always mixes the two frames.

Between two points

A note seldom lands exactly on the points of a frame. The reader takes four points around the position and draws a curve through them (4-point Hermite, also called Catmull-Rom). Linear interpolation draws a straight line between two points. The Hermite curve is smoother, and it keeps more of the copies of the spectrum (images) away from the sound.

The response of the two methods. At the table's own sample rate (1 on the axis) and above are the images. The shipped reader uses Hermite on every pool table and on Prim. and Harm.

Aliasing: the mip levels

A bright frame has many harmonics. For a high note, the top harmonics go past half the sample rate (24 kHz). They fold back as tones that are not in the table: this is aliasing. To prevent this, the DSP keeps each frame several times, as mip levels:

  • Level 0 is the frame. Each level after it keeps half the harmonics of the level before it. The DSP removes the harmonics with a Fourier transform, which is exact for a cycle. The harmonics that stay keep their level and their phase.
  • Each level has at least 8 points for each cycle of its highest harmonic.
  • For each block, the reader chooses the lowest level whose highest harmonic stays below 28 kHz at the note's frequency. A harmonic between 24 and 28 kHz folds back to 20 to 24 kHz, above the range of hearing. The worst case is a note just above a change of level: its harmonics reach about 14 kHz.

Changes without clicks: DCLK

A sudden change of the wave gives a click. DCLK (page 4) is a crossfade for these changes, for each oscillator. A change is sudden when, from one block to the next:

  • POS moves by more than 2 frames, or
  • the oscillator plays a different table, or
  • the gain changes by more than 0.1. At TRIM 0 dB, this is 10 LEV steps. At the default TRIM of −11 dB, it is 36 LEV steps.

Then the oscillator plays the old settings and the new settings together, at the same phase. The old settings fade out and the new settings fade in, in a straight line over the DCLK time. A smaller change does not start a crossfade.

A sudden change during a crossfade waits until the crossfade ends. Thus, with a long DCLK, a fast LFO on TBL changes table at the speed of the crossfade. A new note does not crossfade. When you replace a table that plays, the DSP builds the new table in a spare region. The old table plays until the new table is ready. Then DCLK crossfades from the old table to the new table.

SLW and SYN

SLW means slew. SLW1 and SLW2 (page 4) are a slew on POS, one for each oscillator. Before version 0.1.1, their name was SMT1 and SMT2.

The slew is a one-pole filter that the DSP steps 1,500 times a second. It is not a slew limiter with a fixed rate: POS moves fast at first, and slower as it comes near its target. Its time constant is 1 s at 0. The time constant halves every 14 steps, to 2 ms at 126. At 127, the slew is off.

How the slew works

For each voice and each oscillator, the DSP keeps one number: the POS that the reader plays, as a 32-bit float. At the start of the sound it is 0. Once a block (1,500 times a second), the DSP moves that number towards the POS that it receives:

s = s + (POS − s) × k

  • POS is the target: the knob, the p-locks and the LFOs, as the main processor sends them, with MOVE added and the result held inside the table.
  • k sets the speed. It is 1 − e−1 / (1,500 τ), with τ the time constant of the SLW value. A table of 128 values, made when the firmware is built, holds k, so the DSP does not calculate an exponential.
  • At SLW 127, k is exactly 1. Then s is POS in every block, and the sound is the same as with no slew, bit for bit. Every sound saved before the slew existed holds 127.
  • When a new note starts on the voice, the DSP sets s to POS at once.
  • The reader then plays s, rounded down to its fixed-point position: 16 bits for the fraction of a frame.

This is a one-pole low-pass filter on POS. After one time constant, s has moved 63 % of a jump. After three time constants, it has moved 95 %.

Changes slower than the cutoff (1 / (2πτ)) pass through. Faster changes lose depth. The slew costs a few dozen instructions for each voice and oscillator in each block. It does not add a cost for each sample.

SLW slews the POS that you hear: the knob, p-locks, LFOs and MOVE, added together. Waverider applies the slew after MOVE, just before the reader. The DSP receives POS with the p-locks and the LFOs already added, so it cannot slew one source and not another. Each of the three sources responds as follows:

  • A turn of the knob slews to the new value.
  • A p-lock on a trig with a note does not slew. A new note starts the slew at its target, so the note plays the locked POS at once. A trigless lock slews.
  • An LFO or MOVE on POS slews too. A long SLW reduces the depth of a fast LFO (the table below).

SYN1 and SYN2 (page 4) are different controls. They set MOVE, the oscillator's own modulator, to follow the tempo. With SYN on, RATE shows a note length. SYN does not change the LFOs, which have their own tempo settings, and it does not change p-locks.

The noise

Each voice has its own noise generator, a 32-bit xorshift. Each voice starts from a different seed, so two voices never play the same noise. The four types:

  • WHT: the generator's output, white noise.
  • PNK: white noise through Paul Kellet's economy pink filter (three one-pole filters and a direct path), about −3 dB an octave.
  • BRN: white noise through a leaky integrator, about −6 dB an octave above its corner.
  • DIG: the top bit of the generator, ±0.5: a digital, two-level noise.

The spectra of the four types, at COLR 0.

COLR tilts the spectrum. A one-pole low-pass filter at about 1 kHz follows the noise, and COLR subtracts a part of its output from the noise: COLR / 64 of it. A negative COLR adds low frequencies. A positive COLR removes them.

DEC is the decay after each note: an exponential, with a time constant of 5 ms at 0 to 10 s at 126. At Inf, the noise has no decay. A new note starts the decay again.
